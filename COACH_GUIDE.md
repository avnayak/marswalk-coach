# The Coach Guide

How the Marswalk Coach helps an astronaut in a dust storm — and why each
step is real.

## Why the coach lives on the map

Earth is up to 20 minutes away by radio. A scared astronaut cannot wait
20 minutes for help. So the help lives on the map, right where the
astronaut is. The coach advises — the astronaut decides. It calms the
mind and shows the facts, so the astronaut can decide. Next step: the guide
in VR glasses, reading the astronaut's cues — so the coach knows when to
speak without being asked.

## What is true about a Mars dust storm

- The wind cannot knock an astronaut down or punch a hole in the suit.
  Mars air is about 1% as thick as Earth's, so even a 60 mph gust pushes
  like a light breeze. (NASA)
- The real danger is losing sight. In the big 2018 storm, visibility
  fell to under 3 km. A walker who cannot see can get lost.
- Dust coats the visor, clogs seals and joints, and slowly wears the
  suit down over many walks. The dust also carries chemicals
  (perchlorates) that are bad to breathe, so it must not be carried
  into the habitat.
- There is no rain on Mars — no water rain, no acid rain.

## What the coach says

1. Breathe. "Breathe with me. In… two… three… four. Out… two… three…
   four." Calm first, because a panicking mind cannot decide.
2. One check only. "What does the map show for the storm?" The map
   shows the storm readout: steady, wind light, visibility dropping.
3. One decision. "Turn back now, while things are steady." Follow
   the white dashes home. The astronaut decides; the coach only
   shows the facts. In our story the storm hits early in the day's
   walk, a short walk from camp.
4. After the walk. "Do not wipe the visor — wiping scratches it. Brush
   the dust off before going inside. Check the seals." Apollo crews
   learned this the hard way: wiping dusty visors scratched them.

## What the coach does not do

- It advises — the astronaut decides.
- It does not shout or rush. One line at a time.
- It does not pretend. Every fact above comes from NASA research.
